A latent condition is a weakness that sits quietly inside a system, put there by an earlier design choice, setting or shortcut, and does nothing until other events line up with it. The idea comes from safety research on accidents in aviation, medicine and industry, and is often drawn as slices of Swiss cheese whose holes occasionally line up. Finding and writing up latent conditions before they cause harm is cheaper than learning about them from an incident.
